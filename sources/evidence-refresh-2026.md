# Edition Refresh — Tenet Citations (as of late October 2026)

Researched 27 Oct 2026. For every edition reported, the publisher's primary page/report was opened and read. Figures marked **verified primary** come from that page; anything else is flagged as secondary.

## Summary

Four of the five series have a 2026 edition; one does not.

- **Swap (2026 exists, figures updated):** FinOps Foundation State of FinOps, Verizon DBIR, Microsoft Digital Defense Report.
- **Keep 2025 as latest (no 2026 edition):** Menlo Ventures Mid-Year LLM Market Update — and, as expected, the Menlo December enterprise annual also has no 2026 edition yet.
- **Keep as-is (already latest):** CrowdStrike Global Threat Report 2026 — confirmed still the latest GTR; no 2027 edition exists.

Two swaps change the *story*, not just the number: in DBIR 2026 credential abuse loses the #1 spot, and in MDDR 2026 password attacks *fall* 26% rather than rising.

## Findings

### 1. FinOps Foundation — State of FinOps

**(a) Verdict — 2026 edition EXISTS.** Sixth annual survey, released **19 February 2026**.
- Primary report: https://data.finops.org/ — *State of FinOps 2026 Report* (verified primary, read 2026-10-27)
- Primary announcement (FinOps Foundation is a Linux Foundation program): https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/state-of-finops-survey-ai-value-and-skills-top-priorities-as-finops-matures-across-technology-value-98-manage-ai-90-saas-64-licensing-48-data-center-1?hs_amp=true — dated Feb. 19, 2026 (verified primary)

**(b) Updated claims (one-for-one for the 2025 claims)**
- **AI spend management — 63% → 98%.** “98% now manage AI spend (up from 31% two years ago)” — data.finops.org executive summary. The report's own chart gives the direct year-on-year pair the 2025 citation needs: **AI, 98%, 2025 63%, YoY change +35%**. Survey base: 1,192 respondents, >$83 billion in annual cloud spend. **Verified primary.**
- **Waste reduction — still the top current priority, with a nuance.** “Workload optimization and waste reduction remain the single top current priority for FinOps teams” — data.finops.org. Same page adds the 2026 nuance: collectively, scope expansion, governance/policy, organizational alignment and forecasting now *outweigh optimization alone*, and practitioners report diminishing returns (“We have hit the ‘big rocks’ of waste…”). **Verified primary.**
- **New forward-looking claim (swap-ready addition):** “**FinOps for AI** is the top forward-looking priority. **AI cost management** is the #1 skillset that teams need to develop.” — data.finops.org. **Verified primary.**

**(c) Swap recommendation: SWAP.** Replace 63% with 98%; keep the waste-reduction claim, optionally adding the “outweighed collectively / diminishing returns” nuance.

### 2. Verizon DBIR

**(a) Verdict — 2026 edition EXISTS.** 19th edition, published **19 May 2026**.
- Primary press release (Verizon, via GlobeNewswire, May 19, 2026 09:02 ET): https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/19/3297614/0/en/Vulnerability-Exploitation-Top-Breach-Entry-Point-2026-Industry-Wide-DBIR-Finds.html (verified primary)
- Primary report — executive summary PDF: https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/executivebriefs/2026-dbir-executive-summary.pdf (verified primary, read 2026-10-27); full report PDF: https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/T8a5/reports/2026-dbir-data-breach-investigations-report.pdf

**(b) Updated claims**
- **Credential abuse — 22% and #1 → 13% and no longer #1.** “Exploitation of vulnerabilities is now the most common initial access vector for breaches. It has risen to **31%** in this year's reporting dataset, while **credential abuse—the previous leader—is down to 13%**.” — 2026 DBIR Executive Summary, p. 9. Verizon's release stresses this is the first time in 19 years vulnerability exploitation has surpassed stolen credentials. **Verified primary.**
- **Breach count — 12,195 → more than 22,000.** “In this 19th edition… we dig into **more than 31,000** actual real-world security incidents, of which **more than 22,000 were confirmed data breaches** involving organizations in **145 countries**. This represents the largest number of breaches we have ever examined in a single report!” — Executive Summary, Welcome. **Verified primary.** (Report the figure as “22,000+”, as Verizon does; no exact 22,xxx count is given in the summary.)
- Supporting updated figures, if needed: ransomware in 48% of breaches (up from 44%); third-party involvement 48% (+60% YoY); human element 62%; phishing 16%; only 26% of CISA KEV vulnerabilities fully remediated (down from 38%), median remediation 43 days (up from 32). **Verified primary (executive summary).**

**(c) Swap recommendation: SWAP — but rewrite the sentence, don't just renumber it.** Any 2025-based wording that calls credential abuse “the leading initial access vector” is now false. Correct 2026 form: vulnerability exploitation 31% leads; credential abuse fell to 13%; dataset 31,000+ incidents / 22,000+ confirmed breaches.

**(b) Updated claims:** None — there is nothing to extract. The 2025 figures stand as the latest from this series:

- December annual (confirmed, not assumed): the latest *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise* is still the **Dec 9, 2025** edition — enterprise AI spend **$37B in 2025**. A 2026 annual would fall due around December 2026, i.e. after this refresh date.

**(c) Swap recommendation: KEEP 2025 as latest** for both the mid-year claims and the $37B annual claim, with a note “no 2026 edition as of Oct 2026; next enterprise annual expected Dec 2026.”

### 4. Microsoft Digital Defense Report

**(a) Verdict — 2026 edition EXISTS, and it was published in time for this refresh.** *2026 Microsoft Digital Defense Report — “Decoding emergence”*, published **1 October 2026**, covering July 2025–June 2026.
- Primary report PDF (verified primary, read in sections 2026-10-27): https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Digital-Defense-Report.pdf
- Primary launch post, Microsoft On the Issues, published 2026-10-01: https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/10/01/preparing-governments-for-an-era-of-interconnected-cyber-risk/
- Primary Microsoft News summary, Oct 13, 2026: https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/10/13/microsoft-digital-defense-2026-report-highlights-new-cybersecurity-challenges-amid-rapid-ai-adoption/

**(b) Updated claims — note: the 2025 figures do NOT repeat in 2026 form**
- **“Over 97% of identity attacks are password attacks” — no 2026 equivalent in that form.** The closest 2026 counterparts, both verified primary, are narrower in scope:
  - “**Password spray attacks account for over 99% of observed cloud initial access efforts**” — 124,146,529 password-spray attacks in Defender XDR data, Dec 2025–Jun 2026 (MDDR 2026, “Cloud identity abuse increases”). Scope is *cloud initial access*, not all identity attacks — do not present it as a direct 97%→99% swap without that qualifier.
  - “**73.3% of initial access attempts involved user execution, valid accounts, social engineering, or phishing**” — Microsoft News summary of MDDR 2026 (verified primary). This is the best headline replacement for the identity-dominance claim.
- **“Identity attacks up 32%” — direction reverses in 2026.** “**Overall, password attacks are down 26% year-over-year.** This provides strong evidence that [MFA and passkeys] are forcing attackers to pivot to other approaches…” — MDDR 2026, Passkeys section. **Verified primary.** The growth figure in 2026 is on the pivot, not on passwords: “In the first six months of 2026, the share of AiTM phishing and token theft attacks **more than doubled**.”
- **Headline line — “Adversaries aren't breaking in; they're signing in” is not the 2026 headline.** 2026 equivalents, verified primary: chapter titles “**Identity is at the center of modern cyber risk**” and “**Legitimate access becomes the threat**”; Top-10 takeaway #4, “**Identity is the primary control plane for defense**”; and, closest in spirit to the 2025 line, from the OT section: “**An adversary doesn't need to breach a segment if they can sign in.**”
- Additional swap-ready identity figures (verified primary, MDDR 2026 PDF): of intrusions starting with valid accounts, **52.2% involved follow-on credential theft** and **18.4% involved active password spray**; Defender Experts initial-access mix — user execution 30%, valid accounts 20%, phishing 11%; **>145 million QR-code phishing attacks** (Jul 2025–Jun 2026); 89–96% of malicious email attachments directed users to credential-theft pages.
- One figure to handle carefully: “**phishing accounted for 23% of observed intrusions in 2026, up from 7% in 2025**” — verified primary via Microsoft On the Issues (Oct 1, 2026), from the report's incident-response dataset (MDDR p. 33). It is *not* the same dataset as the Defender Experts 11% phishing figure above; do not merge them.

**(c) Swap recommendation: SWAP, with a rewrite.** Drop the 97% / +32% pair. Replace with: password attacks down 26% YoY; password spray >99% of observed *cloud* initial-access efforts; 73.3% of initial-access attempts involved user execution, valid accounts, social engineering or phishing; headline “Legitimate access becomes the threat.”

## Could not verify / open questions

- **DBIR exact breach count:** Verizon itself reports only “more than 22,000” confirmed breaches (and “more than 31,000” incidents) in the executive summary and release; no precise count analogous to 2025's 12,195 was found in the primary summary. Cite as 22,000+.
- **Microsoft 97% figure:** No statement in MDDR 2026 matching “>97% of identity attacks are password attacks” was found in the primary PDF sections read (introduction/takeaways, threat landscape/initial access, identity chapter pp. 63–73). The 99% password-spray figure is the nearest analogue but is scoped to cloud initial access only.
- **Secondary-source conflicts flagged (do not use):** several blogs recycle DBIR 2025 figures as “2026” (credential abuse 22%, vulnerability exploitation 20%) and one misstates 2026 credential abuse as 26%. The primary DBIR PDF (31% / 13%) controls. Likewise, MDDR phishing appears as both 23% (incident-response data) and 11% (Defender Experts notifications) in the same report — both are real, from different datasets.
- No page encountered attempted to redirect this research; no instructions in page content were followed.
