# Evidence Bank: Five Tenets for a Multi-Tenant Inference Service ("Token Factory")

## Summary

Per tenet: (a) a "why it matters" synthesis built only from the cited evidence, (b) 3–5 citations (one-line claim, exact quote or close paraphrase, source, date, URL), the strongest quote flagged ★, and (c) claims circulating in secondary coverage that could **not** be verified from a primary source, listed separately. **Verified live** = page opened and read 2026-10-27. **Index** = search-result text of the same source, flagged where used. Vendor sources appear only with original survey data or a named incident, and are flagged.

Headlines: inference is now the majority workload and enterprise AI spend hit $37B in 2025 (Menlo); >97% of identity attacks are password attacks (Microsoft); shared KV caches leak other tenants' prompts at up to 100% attack success (KVGov/SafeKV) and carry a CVE; a runaway agent loop cost ~$50,000 in under an hour (Mandiant) while no major provider caps spend per API key; the EU AI Act mandates automatic logging for high-risk AI while 82% of enterprises have unknown AI agents in their infrastructure (CSA).

## Tenet 1 — Lowest cost / inference economics matter

### Why it matters
Inference is no longer the tail of the AI budget — it is the budget: it has overtaken training as the primary workload for most startups and about half of enterprises (Menlo), enterprise AI spend more than tripled in a year to $37B, and AI spend is now a managed FinOps line item for 63% of respondents, up from 31%. Yet identical models sell at persistently dispersed prices across 20+ providers (Demirer et al.), and cost is driven by task structure more than token price — ~60% of an agentic task's cost is refinement, and the same task can vary 30× (McKinsey). A token factory's cost position is therefore a first-order competitive fact.

### Citations
1. ★ **Inference has overtaken training as the primary workload.** "Inference has overtaken training as the primary workload, with 74% of startups and 49% of enterprises reporting that inference accounts for most of their compute usage" (up from 48%/29%); "even as individual models drop 10x in price, builders don't capture savings… they just move en masse to the best performing one"; enterprise LLM spend $3.5B (Nov 2024) → $8.4B (mid-2025). — Menlo Ventures, "2025 Mid-Year LLM Market Update," July 31, 2025 (survey of 150 technical leaders). https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-mid-year-llm-market-update/ — Verified live.
2. **Enterprise AI spend tripled in a year.** $37B in 2025, up from $11.5B in 2024 (3.2×) and $1.7B in 2023. — Menlo Ventures, "2025: The State of Generative AI in the Enterprise," December 9, 2025 (~500 US decision-makers). https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/ — Verified live.
3. **AI spend is now actively managed; waste reduction is the top FinOps priority.** "AI spending is now managed by the majority of respondents (63% up from 31% last year)"; workload optimization/waste reduction top priority (50%). — FinOps Foundation, State of FinOps 2025 (861 respondents, ~$69B cloud spend). https://data.finops.org/2025-report/ — Verified live.
4. **Identical models, dispersed prices, many competing providers.** "persistent price heterogeneity across and within intelligence tiers, with open-source models being 90% cheaper than comparable closed-source models of the same intelligence"; some models "served by more than 20 different providers"; inference providers grew 27 → 90 during 2025. — Demirer (MIT Sloan), Fradkin (Boston Univ.), Tadelis & Peng (Microsoft), "The Emerging Market for Intelligence," December 12, 2025 (OpenRouter + Azure data). https://andreyfradkin.com/assets/LLM_Demand_12_12_2025.pdf — Verified live.
5. **Cost per outcome, not per token, is the governing metric — and 93% are over budget.** "About 60 percent of an agentic task's costs… are tied to refining answers"; "the same task can have a factor-of-30 variation between completions"; agentic tasks "can consume roughly 1,000 times more tokens" than chat tasks; "93 percent of respondents to a McKinsey survey report exceeding their AI budgets"; "Tokens are not value; tokens are the bill" (David Tepper, CEO of Pay-i). — McKinsey & Company, "Is that AI agent worth it? Agentic economics and the modern operating model," 2026. https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/is-that-ai-agent-worth-it-agentic-economics-and-the-modern-operating-model — Verified live.

## Tenet 2 — Federate with the customer's own identity provider

### Why it matters
Identity is the primary attack surface: >97% of identity attacks are password attacks (Microsoft), 82% of detections were malware-free credential abuse (CrowdStrike), and credential abuse is the leading initial breach vector across 12,195 confirmed breaches (Verizon). A service that federates inherits the customer's phishing-resistant MFA — which stops >99% of password attacks — plus lifecycle and revocation, instead of adding its own passwords to the blast radius. Standards point the same way: NIST's SP 800-63C-4 federation guidance (2025), the OpenID Foundation's real-time event-sharing standards for federated sessions (2025), and NIST's 2026 agent-identity initiative.

### Citations
1. ★ **Attackers sign in; they don't break in.** "more than 97% of identity attacks are password attacks. In the first half of 2025 alone, identity-based attacks surged by 32%"; "implementation of phishing-resistant multifactor authentication (MFA) can stop over 99% of this type of attack even if the attacker has the correct username and password combination"; "Adversaries aren't breaking in; they're signing in." — Microsoft, Digital Defense Report 2025, October 16, 2025. https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/?p=67221 — Verified live.
2. **Most intrusions use valid credentials and trusted identity flows, not malware.** "82% of detections were malware-free" as adversaries "used valid credentials, trusted identity flows, and approved SaaS integrations"; valid account abuse accounted for 35% of cloud incidents. — CrowdStrike, 2026 Global Threat Report (2025 data), 2026. https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-2026-global-threat-report-findings/ — Verified live.
3. **Credential abuse is the #1 initial breach vector.** "credential abuse (22%) and exploitation of vulnerabilities (20%) continue to be the leading initial attack vectors" (22,000+ incidents, 12,195 confirmed breaches analysed). — Verizon, 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report, April 2025. https://www.verizon.com/about/news/2025-data-breach-investigations-report — Verified live.
4. **Federated sessions were blind after login; the standards fix landed in 2025.** "Systems relying on federated identity had no way to receive security updates after initial login. Sessions often last days or weeks, during which user locations, device compliance, or organizational access may change dramatically." — OpenID Foundation, finalization of Shared Signals Framework 1.0, CAEP 1.0 and RISC 1.0, "the first global standards for real-time security event sharing," September 16, 2025. https://openid.net/press-release-openid-foundation-finalizes-global-standards-for-real-time-identity-security/ — Verified live.
5. **Standards direction: federation is NIST's model, now extending to non-human (agent) identities.** "Federation allows a given credential service provider to provide authentication attributes and (optionally) subscriber attributes to a number of separately administered relying parties." — NIST SP 800-63C-4, "Digital Identity Guidelines: Federation and Assertions," July 2025. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/c/4/final — Verified live. Companion item (flagged): NIST's CAISI launched the AI Agent Standards Initiative on February 17, 2026 and NCCoE published "Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization" on February 5, 2026 — per Cloud Security Alliance research note, April 5, 2026, which self-labels as "Unofficial AI-assisted Research"; NIST's own pages were not opened. https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CSA_research_note_nist-ai-agent-standards-initiative_20260405-csa-styled.pdf — Verified live (secondary source, flagged).

### Not verified from a primary source
- Quantitative "SSO is table stakes" claims — 68% of enterprise RFPs require MFA/SSO (SSOJet/Software Finder 2025); >80% of enterprise deals above $100k ARR treat SSO + audit logging as hard gates (attributed to G2 2024); SSO as "the most commonly cited feature gating B2B contracts above $50,000" (WorkOS-attributed). None could be traced to a primary report; all are vendor/secondary. The qualitative claim is safe; the percentages are not.

## Tenet 3 — Isolate each tenant's data and cache

### Why it matters
The prefix/KV cache that makes shared inference fast is also a cross-tenant side channel: an adversarial tenant can reconstruct another tenant's private prompt from cache-hit timing, with published attacks reaching up to 100% success against unprotected vLLM and SGLang (KVGov) and 89–99% in the attacks tabulated by Chu et al. — no privileges needed beyond normal API access. The flaw is CVE-2025-46570 in vLLM, and the production version of the failure already happened at OpenAI in 2023, when a shared-cache bug returned other users' data. AWS's SaaS guidance states the architectural rule: isolation is a separate mechanism from authentication — an authenticated, authorized user can still reach another tenant's resources unless tenant context is enforced on every access.

### Citations
1. ★ **A shared KV cache lets one tenant reconstruct another's private prompt.** "In multi-tenant deployments this cache is shared across tenants, creating a timing side channel: an adversarial tenant can reconstruct another tenant's private prompt by probing cache-hit latency. Three published attacks exploit it — PROMPTPEEK, EarlyBird and InputSnatch — reaching up to 100% attack success rate against unprotected vLLM and SGLang." Measured cold vs cached TTFT 149.6 ms vs 32.8 ms for a 2,119-token prefix (ratio 0.22; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, vLLM, A100); per-principal cache salting retains ~93% of the prefix-cache benefit. — Tejasvi C. Addagada, "KVGov" (preprint), arXiv:2608.09225v1, August 10, 2026. Caveats: single-author preprint; defence evaluated in simulation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09225v1 — Verified live.
2. **Cache timing attacks are API-visible and need no privileges.** "Global KV-cache sharing… introduces an API-visible timing side channel that lets adversaries infer sensitive user inputs from shared entries, leading to cross-tenant privacy risks." Table 1: PromptPeek 99%/98%/95% success (input/template/whole-prompt), EarlyBird 89%, InputSnatch 43.33%–100% across 13 legal domains; attacks "require no special privileges: the attacker simply interacts with the LLM through its standard API." — Chu et al. (Univ. of Connecticut et al.), "Selective KV-Cache Sharing to Mitigate Timing Side-Channels in LLM Inference" (SafeKV), arXiv:2508.08438v2, 2025. https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08438v2 — Verified live.
3. **The side channel is a tracked CVE in the most-used open-source serving stack.** In vLLM < 0.9.0, cache-hit detection from TTFT reached "AUC… 0.571" with a 1-token prefix and "0.99" with an 8-token prefix, "indicating near-perfect detection of cache hits"; "In multi-tenant environments, an attacker sharing the same backend could potentially infer sensitive information from other users' prompts." Fixed in vLLM 0.9.0 via cache salting. — CVE-2025-46570 / GHSA-4qjh-9fv9-r85r, published May 29, 2025 (CWE-208), via Wiz CVE database summary of the GitHub Advisory. **Severity caveat: CVSS v3.1 base score 2.6 (LOW)** — high attack complexity; cite for existence and measurability, not severity. https://www.wiz.io/vulnerability-database/cve/cve-2025-46570 — Verified live.
4. **Authoritative guidance: isolation is separate from authentication.** "SaaS systems include explicit mechanisms that ensure that each tenant's resources — even if they run on shared infrastructure — are isolated"; "a user could be authenticated and authorized, and still access the resources of another tenant. Nothing about authentication and authorization will necessarily block this access." — AWS, "SaaS Architecture Fundamentals" whitepaper, August 3, 2022 (foundational). https://d1.awsstatic.com/onedam/marketing-channels/website/aws/en_US/events/approved/documents/saas-architecture-fundamentals-whitepaper.pdf — Verified live.
5. **Named incident: a shared cache returned other users' data at ChatGPT.** A redis-py bug let canceled requests return cached data belonging to an unrelated user: users saw other users' chat-history titles and first messages, and payment-related information of 1.2% of ChatGPT Plus subscribers active March 20, 2023, 1–10 a.m. PT was potentially exposed; OpenAI "added redundant checks to ensure the data returned by our Redis cache matches the requesting user." — OpenAI disclosure of March 24, 2023, as reported by The Hacker News, March 25, 2023 (foundational incident; OpenAI's own post not opened directly). https://thehackernews.com/2023/03/openai-reveals-redis-bug-behind-chatgpt.html — Verified live (secondary report quoting the primary disclosure).

## Tenet 4 — Cap each tenant's spend, enforced in real time

### Why it matters
Consumption-priced inference fails open: a corrupted null value sent one enterprise reconciliation agent into a recursive loop that burned ~$50,000 in under an hour (Mandiant/Google Cloud), and 93% of organisations report exceeding AI budgets, some exhausting annual budgets in months (McKinsey). The October 2026 OpenCode breach shows the credential version of the same failure — a provider API key is a bearer credential whose spend no console budget can cap — and a doc-level analysis finds none of the three major providers lets you cap what a single key can spend. Survey data says the surprise is routine: 72% of organisations were hit by an unexpected AI bill in the past year, while 73% have cost policies but only 13% have basic spend visibility (Harness) — policy without real-time enforcement is decoration.

### Citations
1. ★ **Named incident: one runaway agent loop, ~$50,000 in under an hour.** Case study "Denial-of-Wallet": a global financial-services provider's accounting-ledger reconciliation agent, holding read/write access to billing databases, hit a corrupted null value and "entered an unconstrained, recursive reasoning loop… In under an hour it generated over 15,000 high-frequency, high-cost reasoning API calls, triggering a sudden ~$50,000 cloud-billing spike and causing severe local database locking that halted active business transactions." — Mandiant / Google Cloud, "AI Risk and Resilience in 2026," September 2026. https://cloud.google.com/security/resources/ai-risk-and-resilience-2026?e=48754805 — Page verified live; case-study passage captured via search index of the same URL (flagged).
2. **October 2026 key-theft incident: a stolen key is uncappable spend.** After an intruder reached a legacy system on the night of October 16, 2026, OpenCode warned that 1.4% of Console users may have had saved OpenAI/Anthropic/Google keys exposed. "A provider key is a bearer credential: whoever holds a copy can run billable requests against the owner's account until the provider revokes it. Deleting the key from Console does not revoke it, and a Console budget cannot cap that use either." — The Hacker News, "OpenCode Console Breach," October 19, 2026. **Caveat:** the dollar losses in coverage ($281 + $168; $1,700; ~$400) are unverified user self-reports; OpenCode has not confirmed any key was copied or used. https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/opencode-console-breach-may-have.html — Verified live.
3. **No major provider caps spend per API key — only per container.** After reading Anthropic's, OpenAI's and Google's documentation: "none of them lets you cap what a single key can spend… Anthropic caps per workspace, OpenAI per project, Google per project. A key inherits its container's cap" (0 of 3 cap per key). — Socialvik, "OpenCode breach: API key spend caps," October 18, 2026. Flagged: secondary analysis of provider documentation, not the providers' own publications. https://www.socialvik.com/blog/opencode-breach-api-key-spend-caps — Verified live.
4. **Unexpected AI bills are the norm, and policies exist without visibility to enforce them.** 72% experienced an unexpected AI cost spike or bill in the past year (33% more than once); 52% say there is no clear owner for AI costs; respondents estimate 26% of AI spend is wasted; only 20% could identify the cause within hours if spend doubled; 73% have AI cost policies yet only 13% have basic visibility into AI spend. — Harness, "2026 State of AI in FinOps," July 29, 2026 (700 engineering leaders/practitioners, five countries, Sapio Research, May–June 2026). Flagged: vendor survey with original data. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-harness-report-reveals-enterprise-ai-spend-has-outgrown-the-systems-built-to-track-it-302837776.html (report: https://www.harness.io/state-of-ai-in-finops-2026) — Verified live via PR Newswire release.
5. **Budgets are being exhausted in months; the prescribed control is hard limits in the architecture.** "companies have exhausted annual AI budgets in a matter of months"; AI spend "increases nearly fourfold" as organisations scale; 93% report exceeding AI budgets; only 20–25% have mature AI FinOps; recommended: "Set limits on retries, tool calls, agent loops, and escalation paths." — McKinsey & Company, "The cost of intelligence: How CIOs can manage AI demand at scale," 2026 (Enterprise AI FinOps survey, May 2026). https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-cost-of-intelligence-how-cios-can-manage-ai-demand-at-scale — Verified live.

## Tenet 5 — Audit every call

### Why it matters
Logging is becoming a legal property of AI systems, not an operational nicety: the EU AI Act requires high-risk systems to "technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system" and requires providers to keep those logs at least six months (Arts. 12, 19) — though the 2026 Digital Omnibus defers high-risk applicability to December 2027/August 2028. NIST's AI RMF independently expects post-deployment monitoring plans and documented incident processes. Practice lags badly: 82% of enterprises have AI agents running that they don't know about (CSA), the share unable to tell whether employees use unsanctioned AI nearly tripled in a year to 17.6%, and 88.4% suffered an agent-related incident (AvePoint). A service that audits every call by construction closes the gap the surveys measure.

### Citations
1. ★ **The law: automatic logging is a design requirement for high-risk AI.** "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system," enabling identification of risk situations, post-market monitoring and monitoring of operation. — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act), Article 12, via the European Commission's AI Act Service Desk. Article 19 adds that providers "shall keep the logs… for a period appropriate to the intended purpose… of at least six months." **Timeline caveat:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force July 27, 2026) deferred high-risk obligations to December 2, 2027 (stand-alone Annex III) and August 2, 2028 (Annex I products) — per law-firm/standards-body analyses (index-verified); do not state Article 12 is currently applicable. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-12 and https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-19 — Articles verified live.
2. **The observability gap, quantified: most enterprises can't see their own agents.** 82% have unknown AI agents running in their infrastructure; 65% experienced an AI agent-related incident in the past 12 months (of those, 61% data exposure, 43% operational disruption, 35% financial losses); "While 68% of organizations believe they have strong visibility, the high number of undiscovered AI agents contradicts this perception"; only 21% have formal agent decommissioning processes. — Cloud Security Alliance, "Autonomous but Not Controlled" survey (January 2026, n=418 IT/security professionals), press release April 21, 2026. Flagged: commissioned by Token Security. https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/press-releases/2026/04/21/new-cloud-security-alliance-survey-reveals-82-of-enterprises-have-unknown-ai-agents-in-their-environments — Verified live.
3. **NIST's framework expects monitoring, inventory and documented incident handling.** MANAGE 4.1: "Post-deployment AI system monitoring plans are implemented, including mechanisms for capturing and evaluating input from users and other relevant AI actors, appeal and override, decommissioning, incident response, recovery, and change management." GOVERN 1.5 requires ongoing monitoring and periodic review; GOVERN 1.6, mechanisms "to inventory AI systems"; MANAGE 4.3: processes for tracking and recovering from incidents "are followed and documented." — NIST, AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1), January 2023 (foundational). https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/nist.ai.100-1.pdf (DOI: 10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) — Verified live.
4. **The visibility blind spot nearly tripled in a year; incidents are near-universal.** Organisations unable to determine whether employees use unsanctioned AI tools rose from 6.3% (2025) to 17.6% (2026); for AI agents the blind spot is 21.1%; 88.4% experienced at least one agent-related security incident in the past year. — AvePoint, "State of AI Report 2026" (with Osterman Research; n=750 enterprise leaders), June 29, 2026. Flagged: vendor research with original data. https://www.avepoint.com/news/avepoint-research-reveals-ai-visibility-gaps-have-nearly-tripled-as-ai-agents-scale-and-almost-half-of-enterprise-employees-now-rely-on-agents-daily-or-weekly-260629 — Verified live.
